Clothing Detector

April 13, 2021

Reads out wardrobe for the visually impaired

❮ ❯

I find myself during the second term of my first year of undergrad and already had several years of programming experience under my belt, but it didn't feel like I was a "real" engineer unless I got my hands dirty with some hardware. I purchased my first Arduino kit and after the excitment of getting an LED to blink for the first time had passed, it was time to put my skills to the test.

This project was part of my first year engineering design course. My team and I were tasked to create a system that could help individuals that are visually impaired to choose their clothing. The approach we took was to create tags with different resistance values that would uniquely identify each piece of clothing. A glove with an open circuit with read the voltage drop across the tag, allowing users to record an audio description of the clothing item and play it back.

An ardino nano was attached to the glove with wires leading to the index finger with two magnets. Once a tag (resistor with aluminum foil on the ends) was placed between the magnets, the circuit would close and the voltage drop across the resistor would be read. If the resistance value matched an existing value in the system, it would play back the audio recording. Otherwise, if the value was a new, the user would be prompted to record a new audio description.
